# Research notes: the Haitian Revolution, Toussaint Louverture and Napoleon

Working notes for the Historian advisor. Dates marked (check) differ between sources; confirm against
Philippe Girard, *Toussaint Louverture: A Revolutionary Life* (2016), or Sudhir Hazareesingh,
*Black Spartacus* (2020), before relying on them in the script.

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 1743 (check) | Toussaint born enslaved on the Bréda plantation, Saint-Domingue (France's richest colony). Freed by the mid-1770s. |
| Aug 1791 | Bois Caïman ceremony; mass uprising of the enslaved in the North. |
| 1793 | Commissioner Sonthonax frees the enslaved in the North. |
| Feb 1794 | The French Convention abolishes slavery. Toussaint, who had fought for Spain, switches to France. |
| 1794–1798 | Toussaint defeats the Spanish, then the British, who withdraw in 1798. |
| 1799–1800 | "War of Knives": civil war against the mixed-race general André Rigaud in the South. |
| Jan 1801 | Toussaint takes Spanish Santo Domingo and unifies the island. |
| Jul 1801 (check: 7 or 12 July) | His constitution names him governor for life. Napoleon (First Consul) treats it as a challenge. |
| Dec 1801–Feb 1802 | Napoleon sends a large expedition under his brother-in-law General Charles Leclerc. Toussaint's sons Isaac and Placide, educated in France, arrive with it carrying a letter from Napoleon. |
| Mar–May 1802 | Heavy fighting, including the siege of Crête-à-Pierrot. Christophe and Dessalines go over to the French. Toussaint surrenders on 1 May 1802. |
| 20 May 1802 | Napoleon's law maintains slavery where it was never abolished; Guadeloupe reinstates it. The news reveals France's intentions. |
| 7 Jun 1802 | General Jean-Baptiste Brunet invites Toussaint to a parley and arrests him. Deported to France. |
| Aug 1802–Apr 1803 | Imprisoned at Fort de Joux in the Jura mountains. Napoleon's aide Caffarelli interrogates him, reportedly about hidden treasure. |
| 7 Apr 1803 | Toussaint dies at Fort de Joux (cause given variously as cold, illness or pneumonia — check). |
| Nov 1802 | Leclerc dies of yellow fever. |
| 18 Nov 1803 | Dessalines defeats the French at Vertières. |
| 1 Jan 1804 | Haiti declares independence: the first state founded by formerly enslaved people. |

## Documented facts that complicate the hero

- He owned and leased enslaved workers after gaining his freedom (1770s).
- As ruler he forced formerly enslaved "cultivators" back to plantation labour to save the economy and fund the army.
- In 1801 he had his nephew Moïse executed after a revolt of plantation workers.
- He professed loyalty to France while acting as a head of state, and never declared independence; Dessalines did.

## Napoleon

- Napoleon and Toussaint never met. Any meeting in the script is invention and should be clearly framed as such (dream, imagined debate, letters, envoys).
- Joséphine came from a Martinique plantation family.
- Napoleon is widely quoted as later regretting the expedition while on St Helena (check the source before using).

## Where fiction can safely go (gaps in the record)

1. The meeting that never happened.
2. Fort de Joux: few witnesses to his last months.
3. Isaac and Placide: sent as messengers and quasi-hostages; Placide sided with his father, Isaac with France.
4. Invented witnesses (a jailer, a French officer, an enslaved woman who lives through 1791–1804).

## Well-documented line

At his arrest Toussaint is recorded as saying that in overthrowing him the French had cut down in
Saint-Domingue only the trunk of the tree of liberty, which would spring up again from its roots.

## Sensitivities

- Keep Black Haitians at the centre; avoid a white character as the audience's guide.
- Do not sanitize violence on either side, and do not sanctify Toussaint; his contradictions are dramatic material.
